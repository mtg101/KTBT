# KTBT: Architecture

How the code is laid out, so you can find things and know where new things go. (The refactor that tidied it up, finished 16 Nov 2026, is in [DEVLOG.md](DEVLOG.md).)

## The layers

The game's one big rule is **the game owns the facts, the AI owns the flavour**: the game decides what's true (who's here, what they ordered, the bill, the clock), asks the AI to write the words, and checks every reply against the facts before it's used. The code is layered the same way, from the bottom up:

1. **Rules** (`scripts/rules/`): pure functions and the tables they use, one file per area, including the menu (`menu.gd`) and regulars (`regulars.gd`). No scene, no AI, no saving; any dice roll is passed in. They decide things (when people arrive, who may call whom what, how pull fades) and check the AI's replies (`validate_*`). Fast to test: `tests/test_rules.gd`, `tests/test_menu.gd`, `tests/test_regulars.gd`.
2. **Prompts and the AI** (`scripts/ai/`: `prompts.gd`, `ai.gd`, `ollama_client.gd`, `canned_ai.gd`, `ai_settings.gd`, `portrait_maker.gd`): what the AI is asked, sending it to Ollama (or offline mode's canned replies), and retrying a reply the rules reject.
3. **Saves** (`scripts/save/`: `save_store.gd`, `save_upgrades.gd`, and `scripts/game.gd`): each bar's save file, older bars brought up to date in one place, and the `Game` autoload that holds the open bar.
4. **The bar** (`scripts/visit.gd`, with `scenes/visit.tscn`, and its parts in `scripts/bar/`): runs a night. `visit.gd` keeps the night's clock and loop, arrivals, what the owner does next, asking the AI for each line, and putting the parts together; each part has one job and tests of its own (the table below).
   - **Tonight** (`bar/night.gd`): what the parts share, in one place (the weather, the stats, the owner's moments waiting for their next line). The scene hands it to each part; `visit.gd`'s own names for these pass through to it.
   - **The night's events** (`bar/night_events.gd`): everything noteworthy is announced once (someone walks on, sits down, pays, a dish is served, a chat starts, the cat moves, a big moment...), as plain data. Listening: the stats count them (`bar/night_stats.gd`: `NightStats.record_event`), the TV captions pick theirs (`Captions.for_event`), and tonight's list of them becomes the diary's facts (`Story.diary_facts`) and goes in the night's log. Something new that wants to know what happened is another listener, not more lines through the bar.
   - **Waiting:** the bar's waits (on the AI, a pause, the reader) belong to it (`_frame_done`, `_pause`, `_timer`), and anything that waited checks `_closed()` before carrying on.
5. **On screen** (`scripts/ui/`: the look in `ktv_style.gd`, the K-TV palette, outlined labels, pills and title-card text, which everything on screen builds from; then bubbles, captions, the diary, the recap, the loading screen, the menu board and dishes; and `scripts/title.gd`) and **sound** (`scripts/sound/`: `ambience.gd`, `music.gd`): pieces the bar and title screen show. All drawn and made in code for now.
6. **Autoplay** (`scripts/autoplay.gd`, started by a VS Code task, `tools/autoplay.bat`, or from the phone through `tools/autoplay_mcp.ps1`): a batch of nights that play themselves with the real AI, for testing while nobody's watching. `game.gd` reads it from the command line (`-- --autoplay=10`, `--autoplay-bar=<id>`), the title screen opens its bar, and `visit.gd`'s `_autoplay_next`, once a night's logged, opens the saved bar again for the next (so each night has its "Previously on…", as coming back the next day does), then quits after the last. **Let the crew run it** is the same in the game: the menu's row (`ui/crew_controls.gd`: the top bar's badge, the menu's row, Stop's question) asks `game.gd` `start_autoplay` for so many nights on the open bar (`Autoplay.in_game`: no running file, not muted), and `visit.gd`'s crew section takes it from there: the autopilot at the desk for what's left of tonight (`Autopilot.plan` from now) or the next night opened from the summary page, the controls that would upset the run locked (`_update_crew`: the PD's desk's `crew_running`, closing the bar, stepping in, the next night, reading pauses), and Stop either finishing tonight (`stop_by_hand`, then `_crew_hand_back` at its end) or handing tonight over at once. Only a command-line batch that ran its course quits (`quits_when_done`); otherwise the bar's handed back (`game.gd` `end_autoplay`). Batches run with `--audio-driver Dummy`: no sound, and none of Windows' audio errors. Its summary file counts each night, the AI's rejections and give-ups, and any errors Godot reports (it's a `Logger`). While a batch runs, `logs/autoplay/running.json` holds its process id and how far it's got (written at the start and after each night, removed at the end), which is how the phone helper tells it's running, and stops it.

Higher layers use lower ones, never the other way round: the rules don't know about scenes, and nothing below the bar knows about it.

## The files, by job

Each file starts with what it's for (paths from `scripts/`). They preload each other as needed (never in a circle):

| File | What's in it |
|---|---|
| `rules/text.gd` | Tidying the AI's text, splitting words, finding whole phrases (what every "doesn't say X" check uses), checking a reply has its fields, picking by weight, sorting rejected replies into kinds |
| `rules/people.gd` | Owners and customers (checked), ages and genders, pronouns, stays and moods for company, founding regulars, the owner's fixed life |
| `rules/names.gd` | Names: Korean order and the usual casing, given names, and Hangul (checked against the romanised name) |
| `rules/looks.gd` | How people look (age range, hair, clothes, colours), how strangers are known by their looks, and the street: rain gear, who walks together, phones |
| `rules/portraits.gd` | Which portrait a person gets from their look (gender, age range, hairstyle, glasses; non-binary by hairstyle), the same every time; the nearest there is without their own, or none; the image model's prompts for a face of their own and its TV moods (`portraits.py`'s words) |
| `rules/portrait_split.gd` | The portrait split in the game (`tools/art/portraits.py`'s, line for line, the same answer): an image model's head-and-shoulders picture cut from its background and split into hair, skin, cloth and lines, each at its light step, onto the 64×80 grid, in the people's layer encoding; how cleanly it split (the score) and which of several to pre-pick. Its big passes are shared out over the CPU's cores, in pieces on the worker threads (`_in_pieces`: the same answer however many): about 1.3 seconds a picture on two cores, less with more. And the reaction split (`portraits.py`'s, the same way): a face redrawn in a TV mood cut to a close-up of the face, its eye whites, tears and blush kept as fixed colours, scored against the calm close-up (the same hair and outline, the mood's own colour there) and the best seed picked. About 0.6 seconds a picture on two cores |
| `rules/keepsakes.gd` | The night's keepsakes at 3am: which big moment gets the TV-style end card (the biggest, ties the latest), and whose polaroids go on the diary page (the biggest moment's, then a regular or whoever stayed longest, each in their own biggest moment's mood or calm); someone's kept face from a diary entry, in last night's mood, for "Previously on…" and the edit suite |
| `rules/socials.gd` | The customers' posts at 3am on 달밤, the game's own app: which (0–3, worth most first, one a poster, never the owner; a quiet night a quiet feed), a selfie, a food pic, the cat or words alone, what the AI writes them from (each poster's voice, what happened) and the check of its reply (post by post: a failing one gets the game's words), the game's own words and hashtags (the canned AI's too), the check any words must pass, and which events want a snapshot (by the same key at 3am) |
| `rules/viewers.gd` | The viewers' comments on the episode at 3am, where the show's rung has them (the phone stream's live chat, the channel's comments, TV's viewer board): what about (tonight's figure always first, then the big moment, the cat's theft, first snow, a new regular, a card taken up, the owner), how many and their hearts, likes or recommends (all from the night's score: they never change it), what the AI writes them from and the check of its reply (comment by comment), the game's own words, and the check (tonight's figure the only number, names only tonight's, Korean on a short list) |

| `rules/night_rules.gd` | The clock, arrivals, how fast time runs, the days, passers-by, the sky's light and the weather |
| `rules/orders.gd` | Orders and later orders (checked; a side the line asks for is added), which food a line mentions, what's served straight away and what's cooked, tonight's specials, the bill |
| `rules/talk.gd` | The checks every line of dialogue passes (teasing that crosses the line among them), what conversations turn to (and the night's shape: lighter before midnight), speech bubble pages, made-up Korean, and the situations a line is given now and then (food moments, table customs, sharing, how someone orders, cravings, how the owner opens) |
| `rules/address.gd` | Who calls whom what (oppa, imo, sajangnim...), and their colours on screen |

| `rules/hangul.gd` | What the game's own Hangul means (captions, reactions, stamps, days, titles, signs), romanised, for its hovers; and the words an episode title's Korean can be made of (with the particles that can end them), as the AI's titles are checked word by word |
| `rules/clipart.gd` | The edit's clipart: each one's frames, speed, where it goes on a person and how it moves, its Hangul; which a caption gets (the kind's own, the siren for an emergency, else its face's mood's) |
| `rules/cat_rules.gd` | The cat: coats, personalities, where it goes, how it's talked about (and the moments for talking to it), and its nickname (a stray's, from the list Koreans give street cats, to suit its coat); stealing a fish cake, being slipped a bit from a plate (by its personality), keeping someone on their lap when their time's up, and answering to its name (야옹) or, more often, not |
| `rules/memories.gd` | What's remembered after a visit (checked); the things that stick about a person and who knows them (the owner, or whoever was in the chat); lines that contradict their family sent back; threads (something coming up, for the owner to ask about next time) |
| `rules/groups.gd` | Customers coming in together: whether a new customer comes with someone, what they are to each other (a couple, colleagues, school friends, a sunbae and hoobae, two close regulars), who the second one is (gender, age range), and how they pay (one for both, a bill fight, split) |
| `rules/bonds.gd` | Bonds: one record for every pair that matters (the owner and a regular, two regulars): how they know each other, meetings, names known each way, warmth, how close (a level), each side's memories of the other blurring into an impression; and moving a bar from before bonds into them |

## Tests

See [TESTS.md](TESTS.md). `tools/run_tests.py` runs them all in several Godots at once (each past the first in its own copy of the project, with a user folder of its own) and puts the results back together. Every test hook is in `scripts/test_hooks.gd` (the game reads them as `Hooks.<name>`, and only tests set them); scene tests share `tests/support/bar_test.gd`, and `tests/support/fake_ollama.gd` stands in for Ollama to test `ai.gd`.

## Where new things go

- **A new part of the bar:** a file in `scripts/bar/` with one job, given the `Night` (and whatever else it needs) by `visit.gd`, with a test file of its own that runs without a night.
- **A new rule or check:** the rules file for its area (or a new one, if it's a new area), with a test in `tests/test_rules.gd`.
- **A new kind of AI request:** its prompt in `prompts.gd`, its check in the rules, its canned reply in `canned_ai.gd` (so offline mode and the tests have one), and a test that the canned reply passes the check.
- **A new switch for tests:** `scripts/test_hooks.gd`, with its test value in `for_tests()` and its default in `restore()`.

## Definition of done

A change isn't finished until it has all of these:

- **Tests:** new behaviour has tests (rules in `tests/test_rules.gd` or their own file, scenes in the click-through tests), anything a test depends on is pinned with a hook rather than left to the seed, and the whole suite passes.
- **Stats:** whatever the change adds that happens during a night (an event, a hint, a choice the AI or the game makes, something the player clicks) is counted in `night_stats.gd`: on the debug panel, in the section it belongs to (HIGHLIGHTS for anything rare, then TONIGHT, PEOPLE, TALK, POCHA LIFE, FOOD AND THE MENU, REGULARS, THE STORY, TV CAPTIONS, AI), and in the night's log under the matching group, so real nights show whether it's working.
- **Logged:** whatever it does during a night shows up in the night's transcript (what was said and done, with the time) and its log (`logs/stats/` when playing from the editor, beside `logs/transcripts/`), so a real night can be checked from the files alone, without watching it. Facts as they happen are noted with `DebugLog.say("Kind", "what happened")` (`scripts/debug_log.gd`), never a bare `print`: they go to the night's debug file (`logs/debug/`, with the time), and only a few kinds to the console (the hourly light, autoplay, the season, the night's files; `-- --log-all` for everything).
- **Docs (checked by `test_code.gd`), every time:** the DEVLOG (what was done and why, with the new test count); PLAN.md (only ever what's left: anything built, even part of an item, comes out of it, and anything the change leaves for later goes in); TESTS.md (the new tests and the count); ARCHITECTURE.md and README.md for new files; CONCEPT.md for how the game works, if that changes; LLM-TUNING.md if it's about how the AI behaves.
- **On screen:** any Korean shown explains itself on hover (Hangul meanings in `rules/hangul.gd`, romanised words in the glossary), and every hover goes through `Text.tip`.
- **Safe to share:** no real people, brands or shows by name, and no private details (names, paths, accounts, session links) in code, docs or commit messages.
