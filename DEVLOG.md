## 16 November 2026

- **Night 3's check:** the opening's at the top of the transcript now, and "Getting ready: recap 1" in the stats, as meant. It found orders in made-up Korean ("makkeureo eomneunjuseyo. Beer ije gonggibap joa!"): order lines were never checked for it, only conversation lines, and the order prompt's nudge to order by the Korean names was being taken too far. Orders are checked now, and the prompt says only the dishes' names are Korean, the rest plain English. And rice was ordered as the food 14 times out of 20 (a bowl of rice with a beer): the prompt now says rice goes with a dish. To watch: whether that's enough, or rice needs a rule like fried rice's (it'd change several order tests, so not yet).
- **Checking two real nights found gaps in the logs:** a new bar's first night (Dalgan Pocha) had its menu "chosen by the game", but nothing said why: the AI's answers while a bar opens (the owner, the name, the menu, the signature dishes, the recap) went to a stats object that was thrown away when the night began. And the transcript started at 6pm, so the bar opening (its menu, its own dishes, the series opener or recap, tonight's specials) was only on screen: it's now at the top of the night's transcript. Also: the menu now changes between nights played one after another, not only when a bar's reopened.
- **A crash opening a bar, fixed:** the drinks fridge's bottles took their colours from the menu in use, and the tent's drawn before the bar's own menu is put up: opening a bar right after one without makgeolli (or in the menu-change test, opening the same one twice) crashed on the fridge. The colours come from the catalogue now, which always has them.

The night now stops if there's no bar.
- **Menus that change slowly (menus, step 7: the menus are done):** from three weeks of nights after the bar opened (or its last change), now and then a night opens with the menu changed (`rules/menu_changes.gd`). What comes off: the bar's own pick that's been ordered least, if that's at most twice in its last 60 visits, and never a classic, a staple, the owner's signature dishes or a regular's usual while they're still coming. What goes on: the game offers four dishes from the catalogue (the same kind, best this season first, a hearty main if it's replacing the only one) and the AI chooses, with a line on why; the game's first pick if it can't. The transcript says it ("The Mul-naengmyeon's rubbed off the menu board: hardly anyone ordered it. New tonight: …"), it's in tonight's HIGHLIGHTS and FOOD AND THE MENU (log: `menu.change`, and the bar keeps every change in `menu_changes`), and the diary knows (it may mention the new dish). A hook pins the roll for tests.
- **No somaek note on the board:** the menu no longer explains that you can mix a soju and a beer: every Korean knows. Somaek's still ordered and mixed at the table as before.
- **Specials and sold out (menus, step 5):** a new bar's first night had beer as tonight's special, two nights running: the specials still came from a fixed list for the classic menu, so at a bar without those dishes only the drink was left. Now they're picked from the bar's own menu (`rules/specials.gd`): one dish, by the weather (a stew or noodles when it snows, something light when it's muggy) and the season (a dish at its best now, much less often one out of season), now and then the owner's own signature dish instead; now and then a drink worth pushing (makgeolli, bokbunja: never the soju, beer, rice, somaek or soda every tent has); never last night's again. Rain still means a pancake and makgeolli, every time. And now and then something's sold out: struck through on the board (품절), marked in the order prompt, and refused if it's ordered anyway (the game's own order avoids it too). The transcript says tonight's specials and what's crossed out as the night opens; the stats show what sold out and how often it was asked for anyway (log: `food.sold_out`, beside `food.specials`), and rejected replies have their own kinds now: signature dishes, and sold out.
- **Quieter tests:** the red "Resumed function '_ask_canned()' after await, but class instance is gone" lines came from tests closing the bar while a canned AI request was waiting its frame: the wait finished after the AI had gone. The AI's waits are now on a signal of its own, so they go with it.
- **Signature dishes (menus, step 4):** every bar now has one or two dishes of the owner's own, invented by the AI from their story when the bar opens: a name in romanised Korean and Hangul, what the name means, a line on what it is and whose recipe, its kind (from the catalogue's) and how it's drawn (from the game's lists). The game checks it like a name (the Hangul word for word against the romanisation, English letters, nothing already on the menu or in the catalogue, the meaning and description in English, no brands) and sets the rest: its price (the usual for its kind, plus ₩1,000: it's the house's own) and cooking time. It goes first on the menu board under 대표 메뉴 (THE HOUSE'S OWN), with its meaning; the order prompt marks it as the owner's own; customers can order it by name or meaning; its Hangul explains itself on hover; the transcript tells it as the bar opens ("The house's own: 할머니 칼국수 Halmeoni Kalguksu (\"grandma's knife-cut noodles\"), ₩10,000…"). A bar opened before signature dishes gets its own, once, the next time it opens. If the AI can't, the game has four of its own (grandma's noodles, mum's stew, home-style tofu and kimchi, Imo's kimchi pancake). Stats: FOOD AND THE MENU shows each signature dish and how often it was ordered, and who invented it on the night they're new (log: `menu.signatures`, `menu.signatures_by`).
- **Highlights:** the special events were hard to spot in a night's stats (was there a table game? did the chorus happen?), so the stats now open with HIGHLIGHTS: tonight's rare moments in order, with the clock time to find them in the transcript (`9:58pm  hwaiting chorus`). It lists table games offered (and whether they were taken up) and played, the chorus, big moments on TV, the cat's thefts, new regulars, two people getting friendlier, the first snow, a full house, closing up early and a cliffhanger come true. It's in the transcript's stats and in the night's log (`highlights`), and the panel's no longer a wall of text: it has a tab for each section (Highlights, with how many there are, Tonight, People, Talk, Pocha life, Menu, Regulars, Story, TV, AI, Everything, and All for the lot), showing one at a time and opening on the highlights. The night's logs now go in the project's `logs/stats/` when playing from the editor (beside the transcripts, and kept out of commits the same way), so a real night can be checked from its files; that's in the definition of done now too: if it happens in a night, it's logged. Checking the first night with highlights found two things: a nunchi game "taken up" by a line about the countryside (the tell-tale "count"; a hint's words now have to be whole words, and the love shot's "arms" and "link" are now "link arms" and the like), and a second offer of the same game marked as taken up by the first one's game (each offer now looks only until the next offer of that game). The table games' stats also count a game taken up by a later line in the chat, not just the line that was given it. Offering a table game is now an event of its own, and a cliffhanger come true keeps which kind it was (the event's own kind was overwriting it).
- **A test steadied:** the sway test failed on GitHub (the lean read exactly 0 after a fixed tenth of a second, most likely a drink served just then restarting the sway from upright on the slower machine); it now waits for the sway to move.
- **The cliffhanger comes true:** every "Previously on…" ends on a question, and now the game knows what it asked. The recap's reply says which kind of cliffhanger its teaser is and who it's about, from a list the night can answer by itself (someone comes back, or stays away; two get closer, or meet again; someone becomes a regular; the cat chooses a lap, or steals again; tears or a shock on TV; a full house); anything else is "other", and the recap stands either way. The game's own recaps set theirs from their facts. If the night answers it (Jin-seok ducks under the tarp), it's the biggest moment a night can have: the red light, the siren, 예고 적중!! THE CLIFFHANGER… ANSWERED!, a line in the transcript, and the people it's about know everyone's been wondering. "Will she stay away again?" is answered at closing. A cliffhanger about someone coming in gives them a better chance of coming in, so it pays off often enough to feel like TV. The diary says whether last time's cliffhanger came true, and the stats' THE STORY shows it.

The night's log has matching groups (`tonight`, `pocha_life`, `menu`).
- **Soju rituals and table games:** K-drama drinking culture, as conditions rather than lines:
  - **Table games:** late on (from 9pm), with everyone in a chat drinking, now and then (once a chat) a line gets a situation: someone sticks a spoon in an empty soju bottle for a microphone; the apartment game (hands stacked, a floor called; the song that made it famous is never named); the Titanic (a shot glass floating in beer, sunk with soju); the nunchi game (stand up and count); 369; and, only between two who've got close, a love shot. If a line takes it up (its tell-tale words), it's on TV (노래 시작! THE SPOON MIC IS ON!, 아파트 게임!, 타이타닉!, 러브샷!…), and the spoon mic shows: a spoon in the bottle, music notes over the singer. The stats count each game offered and taken up.
  - **Rituals the game does itself:** a new bottle of soju gets the tornado (swirled hard before it's opened), the first drink of someone's night now and then a "Kya~!" bubble, and a younger customer talking to someone ten or more years older now and then turns away to drink, as manners say (once a night each). Counted in the stats' new Rituals line.
  - **A fix from the first night:** no game came up at all: it needed everyone two drinks in, and most people order one bottle and nurse it. Now one each will do, a little more often; and a Kya~! goes in the transcript now and then, not only in a bubble.
  - **Later:** drinking too much (dozing on the counter, the 2am call), with a content setting first; the bill fight, once customers come in together.
- **Each bar's own menu, begun (steps 1 to 3 and 6 of 7: see PLAN.md):**
  - **The menu as data:** `Menu` now reads the open bar's menu (`Menu.use`, when a bar opens; saved with the bar as its dishes in full), or the classic menu (today's thirteen, which old bars and the tests keep). Everything that asks it (orders, the bill, the prompts, the glossary, the dish drawings, the canned AI, tonight's specials) gets that bar's, and the glossary rebuilds its tables when the menu changes (`Menu.version`).   - **The catalogue:** `rules/catalogue.gd`, 50 real pocha dishes and drinks (the classic thirteen, plus stews and soups, pancakes, noodles, seafood, the grill and the fryer, sides, rice, fruit wines and a soda), each with its Hangul, description, category, price range, tags, season, other names and drawing (with a stone pot, clams, tofu and squid strips new to the drawings). Each is marked by layer: **always** (soju, beer, rice), the **classics** (most bars have at least four of tteokbokki, a fish cake, sundae, a pancake, gyeran-mari and a late-night noodle) and **own** (the rest, to suit the owner), so every tent's recognisably a pocha, with its owner in the rest. Another bar's dish can be talked about, and explains itself on hover, but is never served here.
  - **A new bar chooses its menu:** the AI sees the owner and the whole catalogue and chooses what their tent serves: which classics it keeps (at least four), three to five dishes of its own that say who the owner is (at least one hearty main), and each price within its range, with a line on why ("She grew up on her mother's whelk stall"), kept with the bar. The game adds soju, beer and rice itself, checks the house rules (the counts, the prices, at most 14 before the signature dishes), and sends a broken rule back with the reason; if the AI keeps failing, or offline, the game builds a menu by the same rules with its own dice. The transcript says what's on it. Old bars keep the classic menu. Also: hot dishes from the catalogue steam, the odeng pot's hover doesn't depend on the menu, and the fallback order is soju and odeng, or soju and the bar's first side.
  - **The door, romanised:** the door calls were in Hangul, and on a busy night they made 35 lines of the owner apparently speaking Korean, unlike everyone else (who speaks English, with the odd romanised word). Now they're romanised like any Korean said ("Eoseo oseyo!", "Wasseo?", "Jal meogeosseumnida!", "Annyeonghi gaseyo!", "Josimhi ga!", "Hwaiting!"), all in the glossary with hovers; and fewer go in the transcript (the bubble's every time, but a stranger's welcome only now and then, a regular's always, and a goodbye only with the customer's thanks), "from the stove" only when something's cooking.
  - **The menu board:** one in each tent, in the bar's own style (a wooden board by the stove with the menu pinned to it, a chalk A-frame out front, a paper sheet taped to the pole, or a lightbox on the back wall; the same every night), with 메뉴 on it. Click it (hover says so) and the whole menu opens over the tent: tonight's specials, the food, then the drinks, each line the Hangul, the English name and what it is, and the price, with the house favourite (the food ordered most in recent visits, once it's been ordered a few times) marked 인기 and anything sold out struck through with 품절. Somaek's never on it: you ask for a soju and a beer and mix it at the table, so every bar with both has it, the menu says so at the bottom, and a new bar never chooses it as a dish. (First tried as a paper strip a dish taped along the back wall, the way real pochas do it: too small to hover or click.) In its own file, `menu_board.gd`; `visit.gd` only puts it up when the night starts and when a new bar's menu is chosen.
  - **In the stats:** a new bar's menu (chosen by the AI or the game, and its own dishes), tonight's date and season, and how often the menu board was opened, on the panel and in the night's log.

## 15 November 2026

- **A playtest review, and what came of it:** six nights (about 115 customers and 840 lines) read through, then fixed or tuned:
  - **Pronouns in the diary and recap:** a recap had a woman regular as "Will he come back?", and a diary had another as "He's been MIA". Neither prompt knew anyone's gender (and the recap's example teaser was "Will he come back?"). The night's facts now carry everyone's pronoun (`People.pronoun`), next to each name and in a closing "Use these pronouns" line; the example's neutral.
  - **Somaek served for the wrong order:** a somaek order may mention soju and beer, so "one more soju" and "one more bottle" passed as somaek. A somaek order now has to ask for it (somaek, soju and beer together, or "the usual"); "somaeck" and "somac" count as somaek.
  - **Rice the AI left out:** 9 of 11 orders that fell back to the game's soju and odeng had a line asking for rice that wasn't in the items. A side the line asks for is added now, if it fits the order's rules (the line wins); a drink or main that doesn't match is still a mix-up.
  - **TV reactions hardly ever showed:** 7 in six nights. The bar for a caption is a 3 out of 5 now (the prompt's wording and the check come from one setting, `Captions.REACTION_MIN_SIZE`), and a check put off while someone's waiting for the owner is asked once she's free (if it's still fresh) instead of being lost: 40 were.
  - **Samey orders:** 67 of 115 orders opened "Ajumma, I'll have a…" or "Ajumma, just a…", and the owner said "You look like you've been…" 18 times. Now and then an order gets a situation (they know exactly what they want, they ask what's good tonight, they're too tired to look, a regular fancies a change), and the owner's first words get one too (setting the banchan down first, talking over a shoulder from the stove, remembering something about a regular); each only when it's true. The stats show the commonest start.
  - **Tteokbokki and soju everywhere:** about 60% of customers had tteokbokki, and nobody had dakgalbi for two nights. The menu the AI sees is shuffled for each order; the game picks **tonight's specials** from the weather (rain is always pajeon and makgeolli; odeng or dakgalbi when it's cold; a drink now and then), marked on the menu and in the diary's facts; and a third of orders get a **craving** (something spicy all day, something to sweat out a bad day, chilled through and wanting something hot, "rain like this always makes them crave pajeon and makgeolli").
- **How real, how tropey: a review of eight nights, and what came of it:** it reads like a pocha (the food-as-love ajumma, hoesik escapes, "don't drive", Jeogiyo, Gangnam commutes, regulars with running stories, the weather holding), with a few slips and a few tropes missing:
  - **Orders get what was asked for:** about one order in fifteen got food nobody said (rice or gyeran-mari added, sundae for "the spicy stir-fried chicken"). The menu's descriptions are now its other names (spicy stir-fried chicken, rolled omelette, milky rice wine, fish cakes, instant noodles, spring onion pancake…), and anything in an order the line never asks for is taken off, if the order still keeps the rules (the line wins), unless the line leaves it to the owner ("what's good tonight?", "something crispy") or asks for "a bottle". The order prompt says people order by the Korean names.
  - **Safer checks:** apps and pop acts join the brands never named ("Instagram" got through); dark turns of phrase are rejected, even said lightly (the owner told someone they'd find "a job that doesn't make you want to jump off a bridge"); and nothing's given away "on the house" (a customer announced free soju for first-timers).
  - **Korean in their mouths:** in 813 lines nobody said aigoo, jinjja or daebak. The dialogue rules now name the exclamations people really drop in, now and then (aigoo, mwo?!, mworago?!, heol, jinjja?, daebak, hwaiting, jjan), all in the glossary with hovers.
  - **The "Mwo?!" double-take:** when a TV reaction rates a moment a shock, everyone else who heard it is stunned for their next line (a situation, not the words), so the dialogue reacts with the caption.
  - **The hwaiting chorus:** when anyone says hwaiting, a few others at the counter join in (화이팅! bubbles, the owner too) with a TV caption: the game's own, no AI, only when it's said.
  - **The door:** as someone ducks in, the owner calls out, polite for a stranger (어서오세요!), 또 오셨네요! for a familiar face, casual 왔어? for an old friend; on the way out, 잘 먹었습니다! now and then and 안녕히 가세요! (조심히 가! to an old friend). Quick bubbles and a line in the transcript, all with hovers; their conversation knows they've been welcomed.
  - **Small fixes:** the first snow is an occasion in the prompts (a wish, a text to someone, the one you see it with); the diary knows that when customers call the owner Imo or Ajumma, that's "me" (one entry worried about "Imo"'s stomach); pajeon comes with a soy dipping sauce (not honey). The game's own (every TV caption and reaction, the diary's stamps and days, the recap's titles, the loading screen's captions, signs and seasons) has its meaning written once, romanised, in `rules/hangul.gd` ("짠! jjan!: cheers!
