# KTBT: Devlog

A running record of what we've done on KTBT and why, newest first. What's coming next is in [PLAN.md](PLAN.md), and AI quirks are in [LLM-TUNING.md](LLM-TUNING.md).

## 15 November 2026

- **Regulars and relationships planned:** founding regulars (two per bar, each with a role in the tent), becoming a regular and fading away, returning regulars kept rather than reinvented, who-knows-whom facts in every prompt, and friendlier terms earned on screen. Then: memories as short summaries plus a few facts that stick (job, family, where they're from, names), the same for regulars who know each other, friendlier terms after enough meetings (the game sets up the moment), and first-guess numbers. Only the build order is still to decide. The plan is in [PLAN.md](PLAN.md).
- **Speech bubbles:** every line now also appears in a bubble over whoever's speaking (`bubble.gd`), alongside the transcript, which stays the full record.
  - **Who to whom:** the tail points down at the speaker, a header says who it's to ("→ 이민준 Lee Min-jun ♂", in the listener's colour), a curved line runs from the bubble to the listener's head, and the listener's name tag lights up. Bubbles follow their speaker (someone still walking to their stool, the owner moving round) and sit in front of the tarp and sign.
  - **Long lines:** most fit in one bubble (up to about 190 characters, in a wider bubble for long ones); a longer line is shown a page at a time, split at a sentence end or pause past halfway, with "…", and a short leftover joins the page before. The first version paged at half that, and turned most long lines into a page with a couple of words on it.
  - **Like a comic:** a speaker's new line replaces their last bubble; up to three are shown, the newest bright and the two before it dimmed, so two conversations can show at once. Each fades a few seconds after its last page. A **Bubbles** checkbox turns them off.
  - **Pacing, tried and dropped:** at first the next line waited until each bubble had been read. With one voice for the whole tent, that took 5–9 game minutes a line, the wait for the owner went from about 2 minutes to 13, and customers stopped chatting altogether (no chances all night). Bubbles now never hold a line up; only Reading pauses do. A long bubble can be replaced before it's read when lines come thick and fast; the transcript has it all.
- **More customer chat when it's busy (from the stats):** chats happened every time they could, but the owner's voice was rarely free. With three or more at the counter the owner now has a single exchange with someone before moving on, and chats are likelier (75% when busy, 50% otherwise, from 60% and 40%), as is joining in (the owner 35%, another customer 40%, from 20% and 25%). The next night: 8 chats instead of 4, 18% of lines instead of 11%, mostly in the 9pm–1am rush, and 4 joins instead of 1, with the wait for the owner still about 4 minutes. A new **Owner free** stat counts how often the voice was free, and how often a chat was possible then.
- **Tests:** 2 new, **349** in total.

## 14 November 2026

- **Names in Hangul:** everyone's name is shown in Hangul too, first, like the sign: "김지호 Kim Ji-ho ♀" on name tags, on every line in the transcript and in the info panel, for customers, the owner and the cat (whose name tag and info line now also show ♀ or ♂). The AI writes the Hangul in the same quick step as the name, and the game checks it fits the romanised name: Hangul letters only, the family name matching a list of about 90 common surnames in their usual spellings, and each syllable starting with the right consonant when the syllables line up. A Hangul that doesn't fit is left off rather than shown wrong (and never stops a customer coming in). Bars saved before this, or whose first try didn't fit, get the owner's and cat's Hangul when they're next opened (from the game's own list for its cat names, otherwise a short ask of the AI), and it's saved. Offline mode has Hangul for all its names.
- **Name tags you can read:** the owner's name now shows in front of the tarp's edge (it was hidden behind it), and the cat has a name tag that follows it round and stays readable when it turns.
- **From reading transcripts:**
  - **Copied appearances:** new customers turned up with an earlier customer's appearance almost word for word ("a dog-eared copy of 'The Stranger'" twice in a night), once even with the other's name, because the prompt listed tonight's earlier appearances "to differ from". With the look now decided by the game, that list is gone, and the AI's sentence adds how someone seems rather than an object (crumpled notes, books and cigarettes kept coming up).
  - **"Spicy rice cakes" read as rice:** tteokbokki's description is "spicy rice cakes", so ordering them tripped the rice check and caused half the soju-and-odeng fallbacks. "Rice cakes" is now another name for tteokbokki.
  - **Names nobody said:** someone leaving a chat said goodbye by a name they'd never been told, because the game named their chat partner. Now people in a chat are named only if their name was said there; otherwise as anyone sees them ("the woman in the beige trench coat").
  - **Regulars in chats:** customers chatting said they'd "been coming here for years", asked whether a seat was free, and called the ajumma's cooking "his". Chats are now told everyone's already sitting down, it's everyone's first visit, and who the owner is.
- **Test fixes:** test failures can be read from GdUnit's report in `reports/` rather than copied from the panel; and a test bar's save had ended up in the project folder (when a broken script stopped a test's setup halfway, its cleanup emptied the save folder setting), so the tests now always know the real folders.
- **Tests:** 10 new, **347** in total, all passing.
- **People with looks stage done:** the grey boxes are now people, still drawn in simple shapes in code.
  - **The game decides how everyone looks**, like gender: an **age range** (customers mostly in their twenties and thirties; owners 40 and over, mostly fifties and sixties), **hair** by gender and age (long, a bob, a ponytail, a bun, short-cropped, the classic ajumma perm; short, side-parted, a buzz cut, a two-block cut, thinning), **hair colour** by age (mostly black or dark brown, now and then dyed ash-brown, blonde or pink; greying from the forties), **clothes** by the weather (a suit and tie, blazer, trench coat, long padded coat, hoodie, cardigan, scrubs, work jacket, T-shirt or knitted jumper: padded coats on cold nights, T-shirts on muggy ones), their **colour** (mostly beige, black, navy, charcoal, cream and camel: it's Seoul), and sometimes **glasses** or a **bag** (tote, briefcase, backpack). The owner always wears an apron, and never a suit or scrubs.
  - **The AI riffs off it:** the prompt says how they look ("in their thirties, with black hair in a bob and glasses, wearing a beige trench coat and carrying a tote bag") and asks for someone who'd look like that, with an appearance sentence that keeps to it plus one detail of its own. So a suit tends to bring an office worker, and scrubs a nurse. An age outside the game's range is moved to the nearest one inside it rather than rejected (it costs a whole call otherwise, and broke the tests' scripted replies at first).
  - **Drawn:** each hairstyle and outfit has its own shapes (lapels and a tie, a belted trench, quilting and a zip, a hood, an open cardigan, reflective stripes, a ribbed hem…), skin tones vary a little, and passers-by get looks too, drawn darker. Everyone is drawn **facing us** (walking, coming in, leaving, and the owner behind the counter) and **from behind** (once sat at the counter, facing their food and the owner: the back of their head, a backpack, a hood hanging down).
  - **Saved:** the owner's look is saved with the bar (bars from before get one from the owner's age, without the AI); each customer's look is part of them for the night.
  - **Tests:** 7 new (look choices and their colours, the weather and the owner's limits, ages kept to the range, looks in words, in the prompts and in offline mode, and everyone drawn and facing the right way), **337** in total.
- **Sound and music stage done:** the tent now sounds like a night in the street. Every sound is a placeholder made in code (no recordings yet, so no files or licences to track), shaped by facts the game owns.
  - **Background sound** (`ambience.gd`): loops that fade up and down with the scene: the city's hum; drizzle, heavy rain on the tarp, wind (strongest in a breeze), and cicadas on muggy nights; snow and fog muffle everything; the odeng pot bubbling all night; sizzling only while something's on the stove; and a murmur that grows as the counter fills. Plus one-offs: cars and the odd truck on the overpass (frequent in the evening, sparse late on), a delivery scooter, the subway (not after 1am, when the trains stop), a pour and a clink when drinks are served, a bowl set down, stools scraping, coins in the cash tin, and now and then the cat. The game's events trigger them; nothing involves the AI.
  - **Music** (`music.gd`): **Arirang**, the traditional folk song (public domain), arranged in code: a gayageum-like plucked melody with a deepening vibrato, a low string on each bar and a soft drone the second time through, with a little reverb. It starts on the title screen and carries on from scene to scene (the `Game` autoload keeps it), playing, then leaving 20–60 seconds of quiet. The melody was written from memory, so a note may need fixing (the tune is a table in `music.gd`).
  - **Controls:** a **Sound** checkbox and volume slider, and a **Music** slider (all the way down for none), in the bar's top bar; the Music slider is on the title screen too. All are remembered between runs in `settings.cfg` in the user data folder.
  - **From listening:** the odeng pot first popped rather than simmered (now many small, soft bubbles at a steady pitch, much quieter), and the pour's rising "glug" repeated with every drink (now mostly a soft splash, only for drinks).
  - **Something to make the sounds:** an **odeng pot** on the stove (fish-cake skewers in broth, steam curling up; hover for its description), with the dishes waiting to cook beside it, and a **drinks fridge** at the back by the stove (a lit glass door, with shelves of soju, beer and makgeolli: only what's on the menu).
  - **Fixes:** the sound script didn't load the rules, which broke the bar scene and made the tests hang; and a variable named `sign` shadowed a built-in function.
  - **Tests:** 4 new (the sound follows the scene, the sounds are made, Arirang's bars add up, and the Sound and Music controls are on by default and remembered), **330** in total. Tests make no sound (`Ambience.make_sound = false`) and keep their own settings file.
- **Atmosphere stage done:** the grey-box tent now looks like a night in the street, and every bar looks like its own.
  - **Each bar's look, chosen by the AI:** when a bar is created, the AI chooses its look to suit the owner and the name, from options the game offers, and it's saved with the bar:
    - **The sign:** the name in Hangul, big, with the romanised name under it (the mix you see in tourist areas), in one of four styles: wood, neon, banner or lightbox.
    - **The tent cover:** one of five shapes (classic sloped tarp, peaked, scalloped edge, flat awning with a zigzag valance, rounded arch), in one of ten colours, three of them striped. Stripes are cut to the cover's shape.
    - **Touches:** one or two of nine little details, such as string lights, red paper lanterns, an old radio, a lucky cat, old photos or a fishing net.
    - **A shortlist of colours and shapes:** left to choose from everything, the AI painted almost every tent the classic orange. The game now offers each new bar four colours and three shapes, picked at random, and rejects anything not offered (see [LLM-TUNING.md](LLM-TUNING.md)).
  - **Time of night:** the sky darkens from dusk to the small hours, while the tent's warm glow and the bulbs' colour change with it.
  - **Weather you can see:** drizzle and rain falling (heavy rain also drips off the tarp), snow falling and settling on the street, drifting fog, a warm haze on muggy nights, and the bulbs swinging harder in a breeze.
  - **Passers-by:** most people walk past without coming in, along the pavement a little nearer to us than the stools, as dark figures in front of the tent. There are a few for every customer: busiest in the evening, few in the small hours. In drizzle, rain or snow they carry umbrellas (some of them clear, as in Seoul). They aren't narrated, so the transcript stays clean.
  - **A loading screen:** while a new bar is set up, the game's name appears like a K-drama or variety-show title card (a thick-outlined title, turning rays, a Hangul caption bubble, sparkles and hearts) over a little tent bar glowing in the night, with what the AI is doing along the bottom. No template tent shows while the AI works: the bar appears once its look is known.
  - **Fixes:** two type-inference parse errors that broke the scene (one made New bar hang, the other crashed Godot through an error every frame), found in Godot's own log; and a customer-chat test that checked for the first line before it had arrived.
  - **Tests:** 21 new (looks, light and weather kinds, shapes and colours, the shortlist, passers-by and their umbrellas, the loading screen, no template tent), **326** in total.
- **Night transcripts:** every night writes its transcript to a text file as it goes (a header with the bar, night, weather and AI settings; every line as it appears on screen; and the night's stats at the end), in `logs/transcripts/` in the project when playing from the editor (`logs/` is in `.gitignore`). So transcripts can be read, shared and checked without copying from the screen.
- **Conversations that flow better (from reading transcripts):**
  - **Topics:** each spell of the owner's chat with someone turns to a topic the game picks (their day, what's on their mind, a story from the owner's life, the weather or the night, a hint at the owner's secret, blunt advice, the cat, or food: likelier just after serving, so some fussing stays in character). Customer chats get topics too. Before, the owner nagged about food line after line.
  - **Out-of-date lines redone:** if a dish arrives or the cat comes or goes while the AI is writing someone's line, the line's asked for again with things as they are.
  - **The cat in conversation:** a topic of its own (likelier when it's with them); a calmer cat (moves every 25–60 game minutes, and only important moves are always narrated); it faces the way it's walking.
  - **Endings:** no customer speaks twice running as they leave (the owner sees them off first), nobody starts a chat in their last 10 minutes, someone leaving a chat says goodbye to their partner, and a chat gets 3 lines before anything can cut in.
  - **English, and only what you know:** everyone speaks fluent, natural English (never broken English, never sentences of romanised Korean; the odd Korean word K-drama fans would know is fine), lines with anything in brackets are rejected (the fake Korean always came with a "(translation)"), and nobody knows another's name, age, job, reason for coming or secret unless it's been said: only what they can see.
  - **Variety:** the game now decides every customer's gender (half women, half men, 1% non-binary: left to choose, the AI picked women three times in four), and each new customer is asked to differ from tonight's others (jobs, and looks: no more four nurses with dark circles in a night).
  - **The cat:** every new cat is a she or a he (a game fact), and every bar's cat has its own name (the AI kept choosing Ssal).
- **Stats fixes:** "wait to be greeted" is now two numbers, **wait for the owner** and **wait for first drinks** (which includes the AI's thinking), and fixing it found a bug: time ran at up to 6× while the first customer after a quiet spell was being invented. A new **chat chances** line shows how often a customer chat could have started and how often one did, to find out why chat share varies (4% to 29% a night).

- **The tent has a cat:** every new bar gets one, saved with it. The game picks its coat (ginger, black, white, tabby, calico or grey) and personality (**lazy**, **affectionate**, **greedy**, **grumpy** or **curious**); the AI gives it a Korean pet name, checked like other names (from a short list if the AI can't).
  - **It goes where it likes:** every 10–30 game minutes it moves somewhere new, chosen by its personality: asleep by the stove, washing on the counter end, prowling under the stools, out in the street, on someone's lap, round someone's ankles, or staring at someone's food. Each move is narrated, and goes into that customer's history if it involves them; when someone with the cat gets up, it jumps down. A grey-box cat on screen (name and personality on hover), and a **THE CAT** section in the info panel.
  - **Everyone talks about it as it really is:** every conversation prompt says where the cat is right now, and the game rejects a line where the cat's stroked or on a lap unless it really is on the lap of someone in that conversation. - **Customers talk to each other stage done:** the counter is now a place where strangers chat, not just a queue for the owner. Mostly it's still customer and owner (about 80% of lines on a busy night).
  - **How long people stay:** each customer gets a **stay**: 30–60 game minutes, or 60–90 for the one in five who's **here to talk** (they also get the owner's attention sooner). When it's up they say a goodbye line, pay and go, even mid-conversation, but they wait for food on its way. At 3am everyone's time is up.
  - **Customer chats:** when the owner's free and nothing's urgent, now and then two customers get talking (40% of the time, 60% when three or more are in), for 3–5 lines. Anyone at the counter can chat, except those who **keep to themselves**; sometimes the owner leans over and joins in, or another customer does. Each chat has its own history for the AI, and afterwards both customers' owner conversations get a note of who they talked to and the last thing said. Lines show who's talking to whom ("Kim Ji-ho ♀ to Lee Min-jun ♂:").
  - **Strangers:** the AI is told they've just met (no names until someone says theirs) and talk politely. The game rejects **oppa, hyung, nuna and eonni** (in any common spelling) in any line, since everyone's a stranger for now; names like Hyung-jun don't count.
  - **Customers in two steps:** first just a name and gender (a quick call, so a rejected name costs little), then the rest of that person. Nobody at the counter shares a name (or the owner's), and the AI is asked to avoid names used earlier in the night. This fixed slow arrivals caused by small Llama's favourite names; details in [LLM-TUNING.md](LLM-TUNING.md).
  - **Gender:** now a customer fact, in every prompt that describes them, and shown on screen as **♀ ♂ ⚲** next to names (name tags, who's speaking, the info panel). About one customer in a hundred is **non-binary** (they/them): the game decides, and the AI can't choose it itself, so it stays at 1%.
  - **Pace (from playtesting with stats):**
    - **Busier, with a rush:** about **20 customers a night** (was 30, then 10, then 16), most from 9pm to 1am, so the tent often fills at 10–11pm and two or more are in for over half the night. At thirty, the owner did nothing but greet and wave goodbye; at ten, strangers were rarely in at the same time.
    - **Time runs faster when it's quiet:** 6× with nobody in, 2× with one, normal from two (when people can chat). This replaced the **Fast** checkbox; the clock shows ▶▶ when it's speeding.
    - **Quick greetings when busy:** with others waiting or three already in, the owner takes the order and serves it, and comes back for a proper chat later.
    - **No more waiting for the reader:** the AI writes the next line while you read the last (and with **Reading pauses** off, the default for now, lines come as fast as the AI writes them). Walking in and out no longer holds the owner up either.
    - **The owner's hands and voice:** carrying food over doesn't need the AI, so it happens straight away, even mid-conversation.
  - **Debug stats:** a stats panel (shown by default; F3 or the Stats checkbox hides it) shows tonight's people, talk, food and AI numbers: arrivals, waits, lines with the owner, customer chats and their share of lines, food waits, AI call times, rejected replies by kind, and more. Each night also writes a **log** (JSON) to `stats/` in the user data folder, with the settings used and the actual rejection reasons, so runs can be compared. With Llama 3.1 8B on the 3080, AI calls average under a second.

The info panel lists everyone at the counter by stool, what's on their table, what's cooking and their bill so far, plus how many are waiting for a stool.

The AI is told what's still cooking, so customers can mention waiting for it but never eat it early.

The transcript says when the owner turns back to someone, and so does that customer's AI context; with two or more in, the owner's lines say who they're talking to.

## 13 November 2026

- **More room for the dialogue:** the game now runs at 1920×1080 in a normal window (was 1280×720). The grey-box tent stays the same size, centred at the top, and the transcript panel gets the rest of the screen, roughly three to four times as many lines, in slightly bigger text. The layout comes from three constants at the top of `visit.gd` (`SCREEN`, `PANELS_TOP`, `INFO_WIDTH`).
- **Later orders fixed:** later orders sometimes asked for more than they got (e.g. "another tteokbokki" when only a drink was ordered), because the line check let anything already on the table through and didn't notice aliases like "rice". Now everything asked for must be in the order, a dish on the table can only be mentioned as "this/the …", and order lines catch aliases ("fried rice" counted once). 6 new tests, **134** in total, all passing. Details in [LLM-TUNING.md](LLM-TUNING.md).
- **Tests stage done:** click-through tests now load the real visit scene, click **Next customer** with a simulated mouse, and check whole visits play out on the offline AI: exact takings, the customer leaving, retries, the soju-and-odeng fallback, Try again, and Ollama being unreachable. To support them, key UI nodes got fixed names and the scene got test hooks for pause length and a fixed random seed. Writing the "Ollama unreachable" test exposed a real problem: with nothing listening, Windows could take minutes to give up, so `auto` mode now asks Ollama "are you there?" with a 3-second limit at startup and goes offline straight away if not. **128 tests**, all passing.
- **AI settings and offline mode:** each machine now has an `ai_settings.cfg` (in Godot's user data folder) choosing the AI backend (`auto`, `ollama` or `canned`), the Ollama server and the model, so the desktop and laptop can share the project. Offline mode (`canned_ai.gd`) answers instantly from pre-written owners, customers, orders and lines, built to pass the game's rules, and shows an **OFFLINE MODE** badge; in `auto` the game switches to it by itself if Ollama fails. It's also the fake AI for the coming click-through tests. Stage numbers were tidied out of the code and screen titles. 12 new tests, 118 in total, all passing.
