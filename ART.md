# KTBT: Art direction

How KTBT looks, and the rules every picture in it follows. What's still to build is in [PLAN.md](PLAN.md) ("Real art"); what's been done, in [DEVLOG.md](DEVLOG.md).

**In one line:** a pocha at night in pixel art, its tarp glowing from inside under a Seoul overpass, lit by real light.

## The style

- **Pixel art with real lighting.** The art is drawn in code, and a local image model makes the parts that need variety and detail (see **Where the art comes from**). Pixel art suits both: a model's picture can be shrunk onto the grid and repainted in the palette, and people built from layers are palette swaps, matching the looks the game already decides.
- **The mood is the light**, not the detail: a warm bulb under the tarp, the tarp glowing, steam, neon across the street, light spilling onto a wet road. The same scene unlit is clean but cold (the style test, 18 Nov).
- **Only the stage is pixel art.** The transcript, the PD's desk, the stats and every other panel stay sharp text in the K-TV look (`ui/ktv_style.gd`).

## The stage

- **624×154 pixels, each 3 on screen: 1872×462** (nearest-neighbour: never smoothed), across the top of the screen, with the transcript, the PD's desk and the stats below it (decided 18 Nov: the stage grew from 380 tall so more street and sky show, and the panels gave up the room; 540 left the PD's desk too little, so 462; the style test's full-screen 480×270 didn't allow for the panels). The tent's stage keeps its 1872×380 at the bottom of it; the street (`bar/street_art.gd`) is behind the whole of it, reaching 82 above the stage. The tent and the people are on the grid too, all lit by the hour (see **How it'll work in Godot**).
- **Laid out from the game's facts, never by eye.** The style test first drew 4 stools by eye; the game has 5 (`NightRules.SEATS`). Whatever the game counts or decides is read from the game: the seats, the tarp, the tent's shape, the sign and its text, the touches (`rules/bar_look.gd`), the weather, the time, who's sitting where, what's on the counter.
- **The tent is most of the stage**, as it is today: it's where everything happens (in the game the tent's open at the back, so the street shows through it too). In the style test: the tent's sides at x 18 and 462, the ground at y 214, the counter at y 166 to 186, the seats evenly along it, the owner behind it. The street shows at the edges and above the roof: the overpass, apartment windows, a neon sign down a building's side.
- **Every bar its own, all the same game:** the tarp's colour (and stripes), its shape (classic, peaked, scalloped, awning, rounded), the sign's style (wood, neon, banner, lightbox), one or two touches (string lights, red lanterns...). See `logs/screens/tents_range.png` from the style test (not in git: re-made by `tools/art/style_test.py`).

## The palette and its light ramps

- **Every colour has a ramp of 7 steps**, from deep shadow to highlight, made by `ramp()` in `tools/art/style_test.py`: the shadow end shifts towards blue (and a little more saturated), the light end towards warm yellow (and a little less). A blue night lit by a warm bulb, in every colour.
- **The light picks a step in each pixel's own ramp.** A pixel never takes another colour's shade, so small things keep their colour (a single shared colour reduction turned the green bottles grey; with ramps they stay green).
- **Between steps, a smooth blend** (20 Nov; a 4×4 Bayer dither before that): the light fades evenly. A dither fixed to the grid slid across people as they walked, so their coats sparkled with the tarp's pattern behind them.
- **The art holds the shape, the game holds the light** (20 Nov): everything drawn in code (the tent, its fittings, the people, the cat) is shaded only for its shape, in clean steps (no dither), lit from above (the bulbs hang over the counter), the same either side; the bulbs' glow, the night and the lamps are all the shader's (`pixel_light.gd`), so the people and the backdrop are lit alike. Before, the tent's art had its light drawn in (the bulbs' glow on the tarp and the counter) with a dither between steps (the C64 look) while the people were clean steps lit from the upper left, which with the ramp's few steps drew a hard bright stripe down the left of every head at the counter. `python3 tools/art/light_preview.py out.png [--minutes 1440]` shows the tent lit as the game lights it, to check a change before importing.
- **The colours themselves come from the game** where the game has them (the tarps, signs and touches in `bar_look.gd`; people's looks), and from a fixed set for materials: wood, red and blue plastic, green glass (never labelled), steel, steam, paper, skin tones, hair colours.
- **Watch the glow's strength.** With ramps, the tarps came out evenly bright rather than glowing out of the dark: how much a glowing thing lifts its own step, against how dark the night is, is to tune in the game.

## Lighting rules (what the style test taught)

- **What's in front hides what glowed behind it** (the apartment windows shone through the tent until it did).
- **The bulb does the work; the tarp glows dim.** A tarp glowing hard washed the inside out to yellow-white.
- **The tarp holds the light in**: the bulb lights the inside; outside, it's the tarp's own glow and the pool of light on the ground in front.
- **The wet road reflects only what's bright**, mirrored and broken into horizontal ripples, fading with distance (vertical streaks read as a fence).
- **The night is cool and dark**; lamps, neon, windows, string lights and lanterns glow by themselves, and light what's round them a little.

## The street's finish (in the game, 19 Nov)

- **Drawn in code on the street's grid** (`bar/street_lights.gd`): sodium lamps hung under the overpass's deck (most behind the tarp, their light still showing), power cables strung across the street in front of the buildings, and the sky: a city's few stars (each bar's own, more as the night darkens, none through cloud, rain, snow or fog; some twinkle) and the moon in tonight's phase (from the date), crossing from east to west through the night, where the sky shows above the deck.
- **The street's own light:** the lamps and the 노래방 neon light what's round them (`pixel_light.gd`: the street, the tent's outside, anyone out front), as the bulbs light the tent.
- **The tarp glows dim:** its bright orange isn't a light of its own; the bulbs light it, less than they light the inside, so it glows at dusk and dims as the night darkens.
- **A wet road** (drizzle, rain) reflects what's bright, the tent's glow, the lamps and the neon, in broken horizontal dashes that never sit still.
- **The old city drawn in code is gone** (its windows going dark by the hour, its hover for each building): every street has its art now; without any, just the sky and the overpass in shapes.

## The mood board (in words)

An orange tarp is the classic; ours vary (see above). Red and blue plastic stools. Green soju-style bottles with no label. Steam from the pots. Handwritten menus pinned to the tarp's back wall. A bare bulb. The overpass's concrete overhead, its sodium lamps. Apartment windows, some lit. A 노래방 sign in neon across the street. Rain drumming on the tarp, snow on its roof, wet tarmac.

## What never goes in

- **No brands:** no logos, no real labels, no real products (the show's sponsors are its own made-up ones, `rules/plants.gd`). No real business's sign or name.
- **No made-up Hangul.** Any Korean on screen is a real word, from the game's data, and explains itself on hover (as everywhere in KTBT). A model's picture loses any text it drew; real text is put back in a font.
- **No real people**, and no likeness of anyone.
- **Nothing a Korean player would wince at:** all the art is part of the Korean cultural review before anyone else plays (PLAN, "A cultural review").

## Text on the stage

- **In a pixel Korean font: Galmuri** (by Lee Minseo, SIL Open Font Licence; `fonts/galmuri`, logged in LICENSE-ASSETS; `ui/pixel_font.gd`), Hangul and Latin in one family, never smoothed. Each size is crisp only at a whole multiple of its own pixel height, so: the words that are part of the scene at three times (a pixel of the word a pixel of the art's grid): the sign's Hangul (Galmuri9, 30), the 노래방 neon and the 마감 on the CLOSED board (Galmuri7, 24); the bar's name on the sign and CLOSED at twice (Galmuri7, 16: a long name has to fit the board). A symbol a size hasn't got (♀, ♂) comes from the ordinary font. Never drawn by a model.
- **Only the words in the bar's art** (decided 19 Nov): the pixel font looked out of place everywhere else (and its width pushed the loading screen's "step in" button off the edge), so the name tags, the speech bubbles, the TV captions, the panels, buttons, the PD's desk and the loading screens are in the ordinary font. A proper bundled font for them is on PLAN.md.

## People

- **Their size, against the tent:** 32×72 pixels on the grid (96×216 on the stage): a pocha's counter is about a metre (123 on the stage), so a person's about 1.7 of them, and the stools are knee high (their seat 60 above the ground). The first size, 27×50, kept from the shapes before, made the people tiny against the bar.
- **Built in layers, like a paper doll,** from the facts the game decides (`rules/looks.gd`: age range, hairstyle and colour, clothes and colour, skin, glasses, a bag, the owner's apron), so a regular looks the same every visit: body, clothes, head, hair, extras, in two views (facing us; from behind, seated at the counter).
- **Drawn in code, in shades only** (decided 18 Nov; `tools/art/people.py`, 54 layers): lit from above, even across with the edge columns turning into shadow the same either side (20 Nov: never from one side); each layer says how lit each pixel is (a step of 0 to 6) and what it's made of (skin, hair, cloth, or a fixed colour), and the game paints it onto that colour's light ramp when someone's dressed (`ui/people_art.gd`: on the CPU, once a look, which turned out simpler than a shader), so one bob serves every hair colour and one coat every cloth colour. The layers have to line up pixel for pixel (the same shoulders under every coat), which an image model can't keep; pixel-art games draw them by hand.
- **The same size sitting as standing, on the same ground:** everyone's picture stands on the ground (its top at 156 on the stage), walking, coming in, sitting or leaving; seated from behind, they're drawn with the standing figure's proportions, the seat on the stool and their legs out of sight, forward under the counter (20 Nov: the lower legs were drawn down either side of the stool, over it). (A first try drew them the picture's full height seated, so anyone walking past looked smaller, and they rose off the ground to sit down and flew off again.)
- **Moving:** on the grid's lines wherever they go, and breathing (the head and shoulders rise a pixel now and then; `ui/pixel_person.gd`), walking from the side whenever they move (a stride of four frames, legs and arms swinging, a frame every 20 they go; drawn facing right and mirrored to walk left: 47 more layers, a body for each kind of clothes, the head, every hairstyle, the extras, and the legs and arms by frame), a briefcase swinging in the near hand and a phone in their hand, the arm bent up to hold it (at the chest facing us, in front of their face from the side, on the side they're walking towards), and facing us again when they stop (to look in at the door, to sit, to serve); four frames of stepping facing us looked like marching on the spot, and they'd glide facing us with a bob without the side layers; the oldest stoop (their head and neck a little lower, into their shoulders); a big moment's mood shows in the pose of whoever it's about (a laugh shakes their shoulders, a sad one slumps them, a shock jolts them up, a warm or sweet one's a nod, an awkward one a fidget); umbrellas and the snow on them are drawn in the grid's pixels as they're made (`ui/pixel_shape.gd`), the phone in whole pixels; to come, poses that need arms of their own (a hand to the head, a glass raised) (slumped, laughing, a hand on the head, a glass raised). Never turned (turned pixels shimmer, and so would their name over them): a tipsy sway, a wobble home, running for it or turning away to drink leans their head and shoulders over a pixel or two of the grid (`people_view.gd` `lean`).
- **The owner's hands** (19 Nov): facing us, the arm that holds a phone does the owner's work for a moment: pouring from a green bottle as drinks are served, a steel bowl of something red held out as they carry a dish over, stirring the pot with a ladle while something's cooking and they're at the stove (two frames, up and down), waving someone in as they greet them (two frames), a hand to the head in a sad or awkward moment (`people.py` FRONT_GESTURES).
- **More of them** (19 Nov): seated at the counter, from behind, an arm goes up now and then: a glass raised to the mouth, two or three mouthfuls on chopsticks, a hand to the head in a sad or awkward moment (`people.py` GESTURES; the arm resting on the counter taken away first). Heights and builds (the look's `height` and `build`): the layers are drawn tall, an average person's two rows shorter and a short one's four (out of the legs standing, the back seated), their name coming down with them; broad shoulders a pixel wider each side, slight a pixel narrower (a plain column of cloth either side of the middle drawn twice or left out: the head, the legs and a tie as they were). Something on the head, over the hair (a baseball cap, a beanie on a cold night, an older man's flat cap, in their clothes' colour) and an ID card on a lanyard (with a suit, a blazer or scrubs). All of it the game's, in the look, and in what the AI's told (`looks.gd` look_text: "tall, broad-shouldered and in their thirties... and a baseball cap").
- **Portraits** (in the game 21 Nov, on people's hovers: `rules/portraits.gd` picks, `people_art.gd` `portrait()` paints): a face for everyone from the image model, 64×80 on the grid, head and shoulders. Klein's asked for black hair and a plain bright red top on flat green in one fixed style (`tools/art/portraits.py` PROMPT), so the picture splits cleanly into hair, skin, cloth and lines; `portraits.py` writes it in the people's layer encoding (step and slot), so it's painted onto anyone's hair, skin and clothes ramps like the people on stage, and one picture serves every colour. Describe a hairstyle by its shape ("small curls all over"), not its name ("perm").
- **Reaction faces** (22 Nov: `art/reactions`, kind `reaction`, `<portrait>_<mood>`): each kept portrait redrawn by Klein's edit workflow in each TV mood (`captions.gd` REACTIONS), its calm picture the reference and only the expression changed (`portraits.py` REACT_PROMPT; say glasses outright, "same glasses" or "no glasses": "if any" adds them). Close-ups: the picture cut to the face before the split (the middle 56% across, from 8% down, 4:5), still 64×80, as at head-and-shoulders size only shock and funny read. The split keeps the whites of the eyes and teeth, tears and a blush as fixed colours (`people_art.gd` FIXED), so a recolour can't muddy them. Pre-picked by the hair and outline matching the calm close-up and the mood's own colour being there; the pre-picks are kept as they are, the sheets (`art/raw/react_pick/`) there to look over if a face seems off. Every portrait has all seven: 882 faces (the ones without glasses made again with "no glasses" said, seeds from 40,000).

## The panels (19 Nov)

- **Panels framed in pixels, the words and buttons ordinary** (decided 19 Nov): the panels under the stage (the transcript, the PD's desk, the stats, Options) sit in frames of whole pixels of the grid, their corners notched, lit inside the top and in shadow inside the bottom (`ui/pixel_frame.gd`). The buttons are ordinary buttons and the words are in the ordinary font: both were in pixels for a day (framed buttons, the headings, buttons, bar's name and OFFLINE MODE badge in Galmuri), and felt out of place.

## The cat

- **Drawn in code** (`tools/art/cat.py`): side on, facing right, 24×14 pixels on the grid (a real cat against the stools: about half a stool's height at the shoulder), a picture for each of its coats (`rules/cat_rules.gd`: ginger, black, white, tabby with its darker stripes, calico with ginger and black patches, grey) in each pose: curled up asleep (by the stove, on a lap), sitting up (on the counter, at someone's feet, staring at their food) and four frames of a walk (prowling, out into the street). Lit from above on the coat's ramp (the same whichever way it faces: it's mirrored), a darker edge all round so it reads on the floor; a black cat's eyes gold.
- **In the game** (`ui/pixel_cat.gd`): resting in its spot's pose when it's still, walking a frame every so far it goes whenever it moves, mirrored to face left, on the grid's lines, lit by the hour as the people are. Without art for its coat, its shapes as before.

## Where the art comes from

- **Big from the model, small in code** (decided 18 Nov, from the first lucky cat): a model's detail survives only on things with room for it. At 24 to 32 pixels tall the cat kept its face, collar and bell; at 9 to 12, the size a counter ornament is on the stage, it was a white blob with ears. So:
- **Drawn in code:** what's small (under about 16 pixels: the touches, bottles, glasses, lanterns, string lights, dishes on the table), where every pixel is a choice and code places it exactly; and what has to follow the game's facts: the tent, the counter, the stools, people's layers, animation. In the same palette and light, so it all matches.
- **The tent in code, in pixels** (`tools/art/tent.py`): one cover for each tarp colour and shape the game offers (50), the counter and red and blue plastic stools, and in `fittings.py` the stove, the odeng pot, the drinks fridge, a sign board for each style (its words stay text over it, to hover), a menu board for each of its styles, the bulbs (drawn pale, tinted by the hour), the neon's board, and every touch (string lights, lanterns, a radio, plants, a calendar, the lucky cat, photos, a fishing net, knives), each shaded for its shape on the palette's ramps in clean steps (the counter's planks a step each, the cover brighter towards its front edge; no dither, and no bulbs' glow drawn in since 20 Nov: that's the game's light), a dark line along a cover's top; drawn on the street's grid at the stage's own outlines (the stage's top on a row of the grid, so they line up), so everything hung from the cover and stood on the counter still fits. The game uses them where they're there (`tent_view.gd`, `visit.gd` `_pixel_piece`) and the shapes where they aren't.
- **Dishes and drinks in pixel art, from their description** (`rules/dish_art.gd` says what each comes in and what's in it, so a bar's own dish gets a picture too; `dish_view.gd` paints it): each shape into the grid's pixels on its colour's ramp, lit from above (its top row a step up, its bottom a step down), a darker edge round the whole dish, a shine brightening what's under it; painted once for each look, kept on the grid's lines, lit by the hour.
- **Nothing smooth, nothing turned:** steam rises in pixel puffs a pixel at a time, fading in steps; the bulbs swing by whole pixels on wires that lean a pixel at a time (never turned: turned pixels shimmer, as with the people); rain, drips and snow are whole pixels; a jacket held over someone's head as they run, an umbrella's shaft and tip, are pixel art; the CLOSED board's a little pixel chalkboard. The tent's warm glow stays a wash of light, not a drawing.
- **Made by a local image model, through the pixelate tool:** what's big and needs variety and detail: the street and buildings behind the tent and the overpass (and maybe, later, portraits of the characters). Thin lines (power cables) come out dotted: code draws those. The tool shrinks a picture onto the grid, repaints it in the palette, cuts out its background and paints out any text. The model runs on the desktop's GPU in a session of its own, never while Ollama's playing, with a licence that allows it (checked first).
- **The pixelate tool** (`tools/art/pixelate.py`, with the palette in `tools/art/palette.py`): cuts out the background (ask the model for one thing on a flat green background; magenta for something green), shrinks it onto the grid in the palette (each pixel sampled 8×8 times, each sample to the nearest colour on any light ramp, keeping its colour and its shading; the pixel takes the colour most samples are, but a dark line that's a fair part of it wins, so outlines and eyes survive), tidies lone pixels, and can add a dark outline. With `--key` it goes straight into `art/`, the manifest and LICENSE-ASSETS. The palette's base colours are read from the game's own tables (`bar_look.gd`, `looks.gd`) plus a fixed set of materials, near-duplicates merged (46 now). Text a model draws is never real Korean: cropped out or painted over first.
- **Every asset logged in LICENSE-ASSETS.md**, with where it came from and its licence.

## The art folder

- **`art/manifest.json` maps the game's facts to images:** `"touch/lucky_cat": {"file": "touches/lucky_cat.png", "source": ..., "licence": ...}`. A key is a kind of thing the game draws and the id the game has for it (`scripts/ui/art.gd`, `ids()`: the bar's touches for now; the street, people and food come with their stages).
- **A piece at a time:** whatever has an image is drawn from it, in pixels (4×, never smoothed), standing on or hanging from the spot the game draws it at; whatever hasn't is drawn in code as before. An image that won't load is an error (in the log, and an autoplay batch's summary) and is drawn in code. Which was which is in the night's stats ("Art: images 1 · drawn in code 3").
- **Kept honest by the tests** (`test_art.gd`): every key one the game has, every file there, its source and licence given, and listed in LICENSE-ASSETS.md.
- **Export:** the manifest is JSON, not an imported resource, so the export presets will need `art/*.json` in their extra files (when there's an export).

## How it'll work in Godot

- **The stage in its own 480×270 viewport**, shown scaled with nearest filtering.
- **Lighting by the ramps** (in the game, 18 Nov: `ui/pixel_light.gd`): the art is drawn as it looks in plain light (shaded for its shape only), and a small shader on every piece (the street's, the tent's, the people's) moves each pixel along its own colour's ramp, the same way the ramps are made (a step down darker and bluer, up lighter and warmer), blended smoothly between the whole steps either side (no dither since 20 Nov: it was fixed to the grid, so it slid across anyone moving). Worked out from the pixel's own colour rather than looked up in a ramp texture (the art doesn't record which ramp each pixel's on), so it's very nearly the palette, not exactly. Cheap: it runs fine on the laptop.
- **Lights are data:** how far a pixel moves is the light where it is, in steps: the night's own (darker than drawn even at six, darkest after midnight, a little less towards dawn; fog and snow a touch lighter, rain darker) plus each bulb that's on (fading out across the tent, and a pool of light close round it, on the tarp, the counter and whoever's under it: 20 Nov, it was drawn into the tent's art; the tarp holds the light in, so the street behind hardly feels it). So under the bulbs the tent looks as drawn, its ends and the street are the night's, a bulb switched off when closing up darkens its end, and when the lights go out it's all the night's. What glows by itself (bright and coloured: a lit window, the neon, a bulb that's on, the fridge's lit inside) keeps its own light. Still to come: the street's own lamps and the neon lighting what's round them.
