# KTBT: Plan

What we're going to build next for [KTBT](CONCEPT.md). Stages are listed in the order we'll build them, and the first one is up next. Each stage ends with something you can run and watch.

When a stage is finished, it moves out of this file and into [DEVLOG.md](DEVLOG.md). AI quirks and how we handle them are in [LLM-TUNING.md](LLM-TUNING.md).

Every list item follows the same pattern: a **bold label**, a colon, then the details.

## Stages

- **The night as a story:** the stats tell us how a night went; the player gets the same facts told as a story. The game picks the facts (the busiest hour, who came in, which regulars were back or became regulars or haven't been seen for a while, the best chat, the memorable line, the takings against a usual night, the weather, the cat), and the AI tells them; the game checks the telling (only names that were there, no numbers it didn't give), and offline mode gets a version stitched from the same facts. Three parts:
  - **Live commentary:** short lines as the night goes, when something notable happens ("The after-work rush has started.", "Every stool's taken; someone gives up and walks on.", "It's gone quiet. Just the rain and Ji-ho now.", "Ji-ho's back, and Hye-rin's saved her a stool."), written by the game from the same moments the stats count, so it needs no AI and can be built any time. Shown in the transcript (or a small panel of its own), with the numbers staying in the stats panel for testing.
  - **The owner's diary:** the end-of-night summary becomes a short entry in the owner's voice ("Rain all evening, and the stools full by ten. Ji-ho was back with that tired look again…"). Richest once regulars have memories (step 2 of Regulars and relationships), so after that. The diaries are the start of the **Journal and trope book**.
  - **"Previously on…":** opening the bar for the next night starts with a K-drama-style recap of loose ends ("Last time: Ji-ho opened up about her team lead… Will the quiet man in the navy suit come back?"). Also after regulars' memories.
- **Real art, sound and polish:** today's art, sound and music are placeholders made in code (the tent, the people, the weather, the ambience and Arirang). Replace them with real assets (see **Character art** and **Ambience** in the later ideas), finish the background animation, and style the UI. Add visual (screenshot) tests once the art settles.
- **The game:** the player joins the conversations, probably as the owner.

## Later ideas (not committed)

Grouped by type. Within each group, roughly in order of importance.

### Essentials (needed before anyone else plays it)

- **Content boundaries (a setting):** the AI writes about late-night drinking and K-drama heartbreak, so it will sometimes drift somewhere dark or crude. A **content setting** chooses how far it may go, e.g. **Cosy** (gentle, no swearing), **Drama** (the default: PG-13 heartbreak, mild language) and **Mature** (stronger language and heavier themes, handled with care). The setting goes into the style guide for every prompt, and the game also checks lines against it and rejects ones that cross it, the same way it rejects wrong titles. Some limits apply at every setting: nothing sexual beyond a kiss implied off-screen, no hateful slurs, no real-world politics, and themes like self-harm are never described or encouraged: if they come up, the owner responds with care. Matters more now customers chat to each other late at night (flirting and dark turns), and most of all once players can type to customers.
- **Speed and waiting:** each visit is 10+ AI calls, so prepare ahead instead of waiting. Partly done: the AI writes the next line while you're reading the last, the owner's hands carry food over while the AI thinks, greetings are quicker when it's busy, and a new customer comes in two quick steps (name, then details). Still to do: invent the next customer while the current ones are still drinking.
- **Settings and accessibility:** one options screen (and an **Esc** menu during a night) for text size, reading pauses (a checkbox for now, off; perhaps a reading speed instead), how fast time runs when the tent's quiet (`TIME_SPEEDS` in `rules.gd`), how busy the tent gets (about twenty customers a night now; a real pojangmacha might get thirty), volumes (sound and music sliders exist in the top bar; voices to come), content setting, AI backend and model (today these live in each machine's `ai_settings.cfg`), voice mode, full-screen, and the debug stats panel (F3; shown by default while we're testing). Plus accessibility options that cost little in a text-heavy game: bigger text, high contrast, and pausing between lines until the player clicks.
- **Older saves kept up to date:** for now, while we're building, old saves can fall behind: a bar saved before a feature existed mostly goes without it (bars from before the cat have no cat, and bars from before looks get the plain default look). A few things are already filled in when an older bar opens: an owner from before people had looks gets one from their age, and the owner and cat get their names in Hangul. Before anyone else plays, saves need upgrading as the game changes (each save already has a `version`): fill in anything new when an older bar is opened, such as giving it a cat, without asking the AI.
- **Packaging and first-run setup:** export a playable Windows build, and on first run check for Ollama and the model, offer offline mode if they're missing, and point to setup help. Without this, nobody but the developer can run it. Later: a release on itch.io.

### Richer play

- **Follow-ups from the Regulars and relationships stage:**
  - **Contradictions between nights:** a regular who was widowed one night had "my wife's nagging" the next. Check the things that stick catch family facts said in chats, and that prompts lean on them.
  - **Terms of address in every line:** watch whether "most lines need none" brought it down.
  - **Regulars knowing each other's memories:** two regulars who chat keep how often they've met and the last thing said; a short memory of each other (like the owner's) and things that stick between them would make their friendships richer.
  - **The numbers:** tune pull, fading and the keen week from real nights against the simulation (the night log keeps every regular's pull).
  - **The owner's closeness:** what regulars earn with the owner is the given name; there could be more (the owner saving them the last of the odeng, teasing them).
- **Follow-ups from the Customers talk to each other stage:**
  - **Nobody leaves without a word:** on a busy night, a customer greeted in a rush can run out of time before the owner comes back, and leave after just one line (their goodbye). Move quick-greeted customers up the queue for their first proper word with the owner.
  - **Tune the order prompt:** "order rules" is the most common rejected reply (5–12 a night). The night logs' `rejection_reasons` show which rule; tune the prompt from them and record it in [LLM-TUNING.md](LLM-TUNING.md).
  - **Invented facts (from reading transcripts):** the AI sometimes invents things that are the game's to decide: food that isn't on the menu ("soup's almost done", "I'll ask my wife to make some doenjang jjigae") and prices or a bill ("comes out to be 35,000 won", "two thousand eight hundred won"). (Knowing what nobody's said, and made-up Korean, are fixed: see [LLM-TUNING.md](LLM-TUNING.md).) Find a fix that actually works (not just more rules).
  - **Customer chat share:** tuned (15 Nov): about 18% of lines, mostly in the busy hours. Keep an eye on it with the "Chat chances" and "Owner free" stats as other changes land.
  - **Promises the game doesn't keep:** the owner gives things away the game knows nothing about ("last one free, kid", "it's on me", a customer assuming "the tteokbokki's on the house"), and customers ask for more in ordinary lines ("one more soju, please", "one more beer, I promise it's the last") without a later order happening. Either the game should notice and act (a real later order, a real freebie off the bill), or the AI shouldn't promise what the game won't do.
  - **Order mismatches:** quantities ("just a soju" served two), the wrong drink ("one more soju" served somaek), lines mentioning rice they didn't order (each a soju-and-odeng fallback), and "the usual" from first-timers.
  - **Reading pace:** with reading pauses off and the AI answering in under a second, lines can fly by, and a long speech bubble can be replaced before it's read. Waiting for every bubble to be read stopped customers chatting (one voice for the whole tent), so if it's too fast, try a short minimum gap between lines (about a second) rather than a full read, or a reading-speed setting.
- **You turn up as a customer:** a way into the game stage: *you* walk in and take a stool, as one of the night's customers (the old **Next customer** test button, now gone, was the first hint of this).
- **Resume mid-night:** today, a night you leave before 3am isn't counted, and opening the bar again starts that night afresh at 6pm. Later, the clock, weather and night's takings could be saved, so it carries on where you left.
- **Resume mid-conversation:** if you quit partway through a visit, loading the bar puts the customer back on their stool and the conversation picks up from the saved transcript (if the AI was halfway through a line, that one line is asked for again). Until then, an unfinished visit ends when you quit.
- **Calendar, holidays and events:** seasons and Korean occasions (Seollal, Chuseok, Christmas, Pepero Day on 11/11, payday Fridays) and one-off events (a big football match on the overpass screens, a typhoon closing the tent early). Game facts that shape who turns up, what they order and what they talk about.
- **Drama incidents and story arcs:** K-drama beats across nights: a confession at the counter, a drunk customer the owner sends home in a taxi, a breakup at the next table, the owner's secret slowly coming out, and the classic: redevelopers threatening to tear the tent down. A light **drama director** in the game picks a beat for the night (a game fact), and the AI writes it.
- **Journal and trope book:** a diary of past nights to reread, and, fitting the name *Korean Tent Bar Tropes*, a **trope book** that fills in as tropes appear ("Chaebol heir in disguise ✓", "Rain-soaked confession ✓"). The game tags tropes as it plans each visit, so collecting them is reliable rather than guessed from the text.
- **Regulars book (after the Regulars and relationships stage):** a page per bar, from the title screen and a button in the bar, with a card per regular: their look drawn small, name in Hangul and English, their role (founding regulars), how close they are as a row of soju cups 🍶 filling up from stranger to old friend, how often they come in words ("comes most Fridays", "hasn't been in for a while"), visits, first and last visit, their usual, the things that stuck, the latest summary from each side, and the other regulars they know. A natural first piece of the journal.
- **Variety controls:** partly done: nobody at the counter shares a name, the AI is told tonight's names, jobs and recent appearances to avoid, and the game now decides gender, age range and look. Still to do: variety across nights (archetypes, reasons for coming, names that keep coming back), which matters once regulars exist. Log any repetition in [LLM-TUNING.md](LLM-TUNING.md).
- **More cat:** the cat stealing a fish cake (a greedy one) and the owner scolding it, customers ordering something for it, a cat that's out all night on a curious streak, or one that only likes certain people. Game events, like the rest of its behaviour.
- **Where people are from:** a home town as a game fact (Seoul, Busan, Daegu, Jeju, a village in Jeolla…), shaping how they talk: a Busan customer with the blunt, warm Gyeongsang satoori (dialect) K-dramas love, written as a flavour in fluent English (the odd "mwo-kko?"-style word, a gruffer rhythm) rather than broken English. Ties into the things that stick ("from Busan").
- **Groups:** friends or colleagues arriving together, taking neighbouring stools, sharing dishes and splitting (or fighting over) the bill. They talk to each other more than strangers do, with others at the counter still chipping in.
- **Speaking for the right person:** in a three- or four-way chat, small models are more likely to write a line for someone else, or a whole exchange. The checks that strip "Name:" and cut off second lines should know every name in the chat.
- **Following one customer:** a way to show just one customer's conversations in the transcript, now that several are mixed together.
- **Long-term relationships:** beyond the Regulars and relationships stage: arcs that play out over many nights between the owner and regulars (and between regulars), such as a regular moving away, a romance at the counter, or an old friend falling out with the owner.
- **Economy:** money, menu upgrades, reputation.
- **Player controls:** set the night's length (time runs faster when the tent's quiet, so it varies), the menu, the weather or day.
- **Weather that changes:** today each night picks one weather at 6pm; it could change during the night (rain setting in after midnight).
- **A better memorable line:** the night summary quotes the longest thing a customer said. The AI could pick the line instead, or the game could favour lines with drama.

### Presentation

- **Bar-specific menus:** each bar gets its own menu instead of the current fixed list. The menu stays a game fact (it drives orders, checks, the bill and the art), so the AI chooses and flavours it within limits the game sets. Chosen approach, a hybrid:
  - **Catalogue staples:** the game holds a catalogue of real pojangmacha food and drink (say 60–100 items, each with a description, category and price range). When a bar is created, the AI picks 8–12 that suit the owner and the bar, e.g. fancier dishes for an ex-chef, seafood for a fisherman's widow. Each catalogue item needs one picture, or shares one.
  - **Signature dishes:** the AI also invents one or two signature dishes tied to the owner's story ("Mother's kimchi jjigae, the recipe her son loved"). The game sets each one's category and price range; the AI only names and describes it, and it uses that category's generic art. Gives customers something to talk about, and the owner's secret somewhere to surface.
  - **Alternatives considered:** catalogue only (the most reliable, but no new dishes), or fully AI-generated menus (the most variety, but small models produce odd or non-Korean dishes, prices need policing, and every item would need generic category art).
- **Weather and season specials:** hot odeng on a snowy night, cold noodles in summer. The game decides which items are specials, so the AI only has to mention them.
- **Menus saved and evolving per bar:** each bar keeps its menu in its save (alongside the owner and takings already saved), and it can change slowly over time: new specials, dropped dishes, a signature dish that regulars come back for.
- **Character art:** the game decides what someone looks like, from facts it owns; the art system draws it, and the AI never draws anyone directly. Since the People with looks stage, the game picks each person's look first (age range, hair, clothes, colours, extras) and the AI writes to fit it, so every character is drawable and can look the same on every visit, which matters for regulars (save each regular's look with them). Chosen approach, in two steps:
  - **Simple drawn people (done, the People with looks stage):** the game decides age range, hair, clothes, colours, glasses and bags, the AI writes someone who'd look like that, and the game draws them in simple shapes, facing us or from behind. Still to do at this level: different heights and builds, more props that say who someone is (a taxi driver's cap, a school uniform, a phone in hand), and moods showing (slumped shoulders, a hand on the head).
  - **Modular portraits (main approach):** characters assembled from layered parts (face shape, skin tone, hair, eyes, clothes, accessories) like a character creator, with the game mapping each profile to parts (a 58-year-old ajusshi gets greying hair and a cardigan; a 24-year-old office worker gets a blazer). The parts library could come from a free pack (e.g. the CC-BY-SA/GPL "Liberated Pixel Cup" sets, licence-compatible with GPL but heavy on credits, or CC0 packs; most are pixel-art or cartoony rather than K-drama), from a paid or commissioned pack (check the licence suits a GPL project), or from curated AI art (below).
  - **Pre-generated AI art library (a way to fill the parts):** on the desktop, a local image model generates a few hundred portraits or parts in one fixed style, tagged by age, gender, job and so on. A human curates them, and the game ships the keepers and picks the best match. Nothing runs during play, so there's no GPU contention with the LLM and it works on the laptop. Needs effort to keep the style consistent, and a check of the image model's licence.
  - **Live AI portraits (theoretically possible):** a local image model draws each new customer from their profile as they arrive, so everyone is unique. Not planned for now: it competes with the LLM for GPU memory (they'd have to take turns), takes several seconds per image, drifts off-style, can't run on the laptop, and sometimes produces something unusable. Worth revisiting if local image models get faster and more consistent, or if we ever have GPU memory to spare.
- **Ambience (sound and animation):** the tent should feel alive even when nobody's talking. Driven by facts the game owns (weather, time of night, what's cooking, how busy it is), so it always matches what's happening, and it needs no AI. The first version is built (the Atmosphere and Sound and music stages), all made in code; what's left:
  - **Ambient sound:** done with placeholders made in code (the Sound and music stage). Next, swap in real recordings one at a time, starting with the ones that sound most synthetic (the murmur, the pour, the cat, the cash tin), then rain, traffic and the rest. CC0 sources: Freesound (filter by licence) and Kenney's audio packs; log everything in `CREDITS.md`. Each sound is one entry in `ambience.gd`, so a recording can replace it without changing the rest.
  - **Music:** Arirang, arranged in code, is the placeholder. Later: a real recording or arrangement (a public-domain tune performed for the project, or a CC0 track), perhaps more tunes (other folk songs, or a lo-fi theme), and music from the old radio in bars that have one.
  - **Background animation:** swinging bulbs, rain, snow, fog, drips off the tarp, passers-by with umbrellas, steam off the odeng pot and the sky changing through the night are done. Still to do: steam from bowls on the counter, headlights sweeping across the overpass, a flickering neon sign, and the occasional street cat. Godot handles these cheaply with particles, tweens and simple animations, so they run fine on the laptop.
  - **Weather changing during the night:** the weather is already something you see and hear; when it can change during the night (see **Weather that changes**), the sound and visuals should fade across smoothly.
  - **Hangul on the sign:** the sign uses the system's font fallback for Hangul. If it shows boxes on any machine, bundle a free Korean font (e.g. Noto Sans KR, under the OFL) and log it in `LICENSE-ASSETS.md`.
  - **Passers-by together:** passers-by now have looks; next, couples and groups walking together, and people on their phones.
- **Voices:** characters "speak" their lines, with a setting to choose **off**, **babble**, **system voices** or **AI voices**. The game picks each character's voice from facts it owns (gender, age, mood), never the AI.
  - **Babble:** Animal Crossing-style babble: a short syllable sound per letter or word, pitched and paced per character (deeper and slower for an old ajusshi, quicker when nervous). No AI, works on any machine, and suits the cosy tone. Built in Godot from a few CC0 syllable sounds, pitch-shifted. The first one to build.
  - **System voices:** Godot's built-in text-to-speech, which reads lines aloud with the voices installed in Windows. No setup and no extra software, and it works on the laptop. The voices sound robotic and the choice depends on what Windows has installed, so it's mainly a quick way to test whether spoken lines add anything, and a fallback when no AI voice service is running.
  - **AI voices:** a local text-to-speech model reads the lines aloud, running as a separate local service like Ollama, with Godot playing the audio. Candidates: **Kokoro** (small 82M model, Apache-2.0 licence, good quality, runs on CPU) and **Piper** (fast on CPU, GPL-3.0 engine, many downloadable voices, each with its own licence to check). Run it on the CPU so it doesn't fight the LLM for GPU memory. Things to check first: how they pronounce Korean names and words, and whether any voices suit the setting.
- **Speech bubbles, next steps:** bubbles are in (15 Nov). Later: a taller stage (or a strip above the tent) if bubbles cover the sign too much, text size with the other settings, and remembering the Bubbles checkbox between runs.
- **Korean terms with English hover text:** let the dialogue use common Korean words naturally (foods, honorifics and titles like *imo*, *ajusshi*, *sunbae*, phrases like *geonbae!*), with a hover-over English explanation. Likely approach: the game keeps a glossary and marks known terms in the text as hoverable, rather than trusting the AI to explain them.
- **AI-generated food art (much later):** a local image model on the 3080 could draw dishes, including signature ones. It would compete with the LLM for GPU memory and struggles to keep a consistent style, so it's a much-later idea rather than the plan.
- **3D graphics option:** a 3D version of the tent (e.g. low-poly CC0 models from Quaternius or Kenney) as an alternative to the 2D view. Keep the simulation logic separate from the visuals so either view can sit on top of it. Mind GPU memory: the local LLM shares the graphics card.

### Lower priority

- **Other languages:** a Korean-language mode (and maybe others), since the LLM can write them. Voices, the Korean-terms glossary and the gendered-term checks would all need work.
- **Player customisation:** the style guide, archetypes and menu catalogue as editable files, so players can make their own version of the bar.
- **Designing the game stage:** decide what the player actually does when they join the conversation (pick from dialogue choices, or type freely to the AI customers) and what changes as a result (mood, tips, whether they come back, story beats). Worth designing before that stage starts.
- **Non-Korean visitors:** tourists and expats, who would naturally use Western name order (see the name-order note in [LLM-TUNING.md](LLM-TUNING.md)).
- **Non-binary owners:** owners are a woman or a man for now, because their title (Ajumma or Ajusshi) depends on it; a non-binary owner would need a title that works (perhaps just Sajangnim, boss).
